	• Name
		○ Zoltar 
	• Biography
		Zoltar was born into a minor noble house under circumstances no one dared to explain aloud. His mother, Lady Elira Voss, loved him fiercely and without hesitation, but she never named his true father. The truth lingered unspoken in every servant's lowered gaze: whatever sired Zoltar was not human.
		
		His mother's husband, Lord Harven Voss, endured the humiliation in public and punished it in private. Zoltar was given a comfortable childhood, but never warmth from the man whose name he bore. At twelve, when Zoltar's unnatural pallor, keen senses, and first flickers of magic became impossible to dismiss, Harven sent him to a secluded academy for the magically gifted. Officially, it was an honor. In truth, it was exile.

		At the academy, Zoltar learned early that affection was unreliable but knowledge could be owned. He proved patient, observant, and unusually gifted at the precise disciplines of wizardry. He did not merely study spellcraft—he was recognized. A quiet circle devoted to Urgathoa, the Pallid Princess, took interest in the boy who neither recoiled from a corpse nor pretended that death was fair. Through forbidden texts, ritual instruction, and whispered promises, he learned that indulgence and decay were not weaknesses, but paths to eternity.
		
		The cult did not demand mindless obedience. It taught him to recognize hierarchy, exploit custom, and use law as a blade rather than a shackle. Zoltar came to believe that most people feared power only because they lacked the courage to claim it. The strong were meant to rule; the clever were meant to ensure that strength endured.

		As his studies progressed, Zoltar began to dream. Visions of rot, stillness, and cold inevitability haunted his sleep: a city beneath a red wound in the sky, crusaders dying with prayers on their lips, and a presence watching from behind a throne of bone. In every dream, a single destination echoed: Kenabres. When his education ended at eighteen, Zoltar vanished. He left a courteous note for his mother, took only his spellbook and inheritance, and never looked back. The dreams had shown him where his future awaited.

	• Mechanical Build — Pathfinder: Wrath of the Righteous
		○ Recommended archetype: base Wizard, specialized in Necromancy. This retains the full spellbook and makes Zoltar's forbidden scholarship mechanically meaningful.
		○ Alignment target: Lawful Evil. He values hierarchy and contracts, but treats morality as a limitation for other people.
		○ Recommended ability scores (25-point buy, before ancestry): Strength 8, Dexterity 14, Constitution 12, Intelligence 19, Wisdom 12, Charisma 11.
		○ Expected scores after standard dhampir modifiers (+2 Dexterity, +2 Intelligence, -2 Constitution): Strength 8, Dexterity 16, Constitution 10, Intelligence 21, Wisdom 12, Charisma 11.
		○ Level-up increases: put every ability increase into Intelligence. It improves spell difficulty class, spell attacks, and bonus spell slots; aim for Intelligence 25 by level 16 before equipment.
		○ Priority order: Intelligence first; Dexterity second for initiative and armor class; Constitution third. Do not trade Intelligence away for physical strength.
	• Favored and Opposition Schools
		○ Favored school: Necromancy. Zoltar's studies of death, control, debilitation, and eventual immortality all naturally lead here. Use its extra prepared spell slots for spells such as Ray of Enfeeblement, False Life, Animate Dead, Enervation, and later, Finger of Death.
		○ Opposition school: Enchantment. Its compulsions are thematically beneath Zoltar—he prefers leverage and fear to pretending that another person's mind is his. It is also a narrower investment against many Worldwound enemies.
		○ Opposition school: Evocation. Demons commonly resist or ignore elemental blasting, while Zoltar is better served by battlefield control, debuffs, summons, and spells that bypass ordinary defenses.
		○ Keep available: Abjuration, Conjuration, Divination, Illusion, and Transmutation. They provide essential defensive magic, crowd control, summons, information, survivability, and party buffs.
	• Spell and Feat Direction
		○ Early spells: Mage Armor, Shield, Grease, Protection from Evil, Ray of Enfeeblement, False Life, Mirror Image, Web, and Glitterdust.
		○ Midgame spells: Haste, Dispel Magic, Animate Dead, Enervation, Greater Invisibility, Dimension Door, and Cloudkill.
		○ Feat priorities: Spell Focus (Necromancy), Greater Spell Focus (Necromancy), Spell Penetration, Greater Spell Penetration, and Improved Initiative. Metamagic such as Selective Spell and Persistent Spell is valuable once his core accuracy and penetration are established.
	• Background
		○ Noble Scion / Arcane Scholar
	• Appearance
		○ Tall and slender, with ash-pale skin, dark hair kept meticulously groomed, and red eyes that rarely reveal surprise.
		○ Dresses with restrained nobility: dark tailored coats, silver fastenings, gloves, and practical travel clothes chosen to preserve a dignified silhouette.
		○ His voice is soft and measured. He speaks as if every word has already been weighed for usefulness.
	• Mannerisms
		○ Keeps his possessions in exact order, especially his spellbook, components, and clothing.
		○ Is slow to anger; when irritated, he grows quieter and more formal rather than raising his voice.
		○ Studies people before addressing them, noticing rank, insecurities, habits, and sources of leverage.
		○ Treats promises as binding instruments. He keeps them when advantageous and phrases them carefully when they are not.
	• Personality
		○ Evil and intelligent.
		○ Highly favor the law and will avoid breaking it whenever possible. 
		○ Puts objectives as a high goal and strives to achieve the goal.
		○ Cares nothing for others.
		○ Selfish but realistic, will self-sacrifice own wants to achieve a greater goal.
		○ Has a deep-seated desire for ultimate power.
	• Ideals
		○ Order. A stable hierarchy is kinder and more useful than chaos; everyone should know their place.
		○ Mastery. Knowledge, magic, and undeath are worthwhile only when they grant control over one's own fate.
		○ Legacy. Mortality is an insult to ambition. Zoltar intends to leave a mark that cannot be buried.
	• Bonds
		○ His mother is the one person whose suffering he does not consider irrelevant. He will not admit this easily.
		○ The identity of his true father is both a source of shame and an obsession. Zoltar wants the truth, whether it gives him power or an enemy.
		○ His spellbook is a record of his hard-won independence. Losing it would be both a practical disaster and a personal humiliation.
	• Flaws
		○ He mistakes emotional detachment for wisdom and often underestimates loyalty, love, and faith.
		○ He is deeply vain about his intelligence and hates being dismissed as a privileged child or a monster.
		○ He can pursue a long-term objective so single-mindedly that he overlooks immediate human costs and opportunities.
		○ He has little patience for incompetence, particularly when it endangers a plan he has carefully made.
	• Goals
		○ Discover why the visions called him to Kenabres and who—or what—sent them.
		○ Gain influence within the crusade without becoming bound by its moral expectations.
		○ Uncover the truth of his bloodline and decide whether it should be claimed, controlled, or destroyed.
		○ Achieve a form of power that cannot be taken from him by age, death, or a stronger master.
	• Roleplaying Notes
		○ Zoltar need not be openly cruel. He is more unsettling when polite, helpful, and visibly calculating.
		○ He respects competence regardless of origin, and may form practical alliances with good people if their goals align.
		○ He views the Worldwound as both a threat and an unprecedented opportunity: a place where old rules are breaking and great power can be seized.
		○ His devotion to Urgathoa is private and deliberate. He does not proselytize unless doing so gains him a useful ally.
